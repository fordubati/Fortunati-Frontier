Oil fell hard Tuesday even with the Strait of Hormuz still shut, consumer confidence hit its lowest since 2014, and the Fed's favorite inflation gauge lands this morning.

## In 60 seconds

- WTI crude fell 3.5% to $89.38 and Brent slid 2.6% to $102.59 Tuesday as Saudi pipeline repairs and a new U.S. reserve release eased supply fears.
- The Conference Board's consumer confidence index dropped to 81.9 in September, its lowest in 12 years, on inflation and job worries.
- August PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, is due at 8:30 a.m. ET; core inflation is expected to hold at 3.3% year-over-year.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-09-30/markets.png)

**Bonds**: The 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.62% Wednesday, its highest since June 2002, even after New York Fed president John Williams said there's "no immediate urgency" for another hike. October hike odds fell to about 50%, from near 70% earlier this week ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/commodities/gold-silver-prices-rebound-as-crude-oil-prices-fall-on-supply-boost-126093000340_1.html)).

**Stocks**: The Dow fell 0.26% to 51,349.92, the S&P 500 dropped 0.17% to 7,670.84, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.09% to 26,797.54 Tuesday on the consumer-confidence miss ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/29/new-12-year-low-in-consumer-confidence)).

**Housing**: The 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to about 7.30%, an almost three-year high and a sixth straight weekly increase ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/us-mortgage-rates-increase-to-an-almost-three-year-high-of-7-3)).

**Tariffs**: New U.S. tariffs on patented pharmaceuticals took effect Tuesday alongside a ban on certain Canadian products; separately, the U.S.-China "30-for-30" goods lists released Sunday cover about $30 billion of trade each way ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/09/29/us-china-reciprocal-tariffs-cut-trade-goods-60-billion/)).

**Commodities**: Gold rebounded 1.4% to about $4,185/oz Tuesday after Monday's dip to a seven-week low, as a softer Fed tone offset the drop in oil ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/gold-silver-precious-metals-wednesday-september-30-2026)).

## World

**China**: The official factory PMI climbed to 50.1 in September from 49.8, returning to growth for the first time in three months, helped by an AI-driven industrial boost ([AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/news/2026/09/factory-activity-returns-to-expansion-in-a-positive-sign-for-chinas-economy/)).

**Japan**: The yen held near 157/dollar. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama repeated that an undervalued yen is "problematic" after a call with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and the two agreed to deepen cooperation ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/japan-s-katayama-reiterates-weak-yen-concern-after-bessent-call)).

## Politics & Geopolitics

**Iran & the Strait of Hormuz**: Middle East crude exports rebounded to 12.8 million barrels a day in September, the highest since the war began, as Saudi Arabia rerouted flows around Hormuz ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/09/30/why-rebound-oil-shipments-isnt-bringing-big-drop-prices/)). *Markets:* this rerouted supply, not a Hormuz reopening, drove Tuesday's oil selloff above.

**Iraq withdrawal**: The U.S. withdrew its final troops from Iraq Wednesday, ending a 23-year military presence as Baghdad declared a two-day "sovereignty day" holiday ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/30/nx-s1-5983426/u-s-withdraws-final-troops-from-iraq)). *Markets:* no direct price impact, but it marks a shift in the region's security posture.

## Montana & Bozeman

**Business**: Joe's Parkway Market, a family-run Bozeman grocery near MSU since 1969, is closing in early October; the owners cite the strain of running a small, aging building as the city grows ([KBZK](https://www.kbzk.com/news/joes-parkway-to-close-after-57-years-in-bozeman)).

**Agriculture**: USDA designated 16 Montana counties, including Gallatin, as natural disaster areas after months of drought, opening emergency FSA credit for farmers and ranchers ([Hi-Line Today](https://hilinetoday.com/disaster-designation-across-montana-emergency-ag-aid-available/)).

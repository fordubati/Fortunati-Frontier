Yields ripped higher this week on hawkish Fed talk and an oil market still pricing in Middle East risk, dragging mortgage rates further from home for Bozeman buyers. On the geopolitical side, Washington hosted Xi Jinping even as the Iran war ground on with no resolution on Hormuz. Here's what actually moved in the last day or so.

## Markets

- **Stocks**: The S&P 500 closed Friday up 0.51% to 7,743.41, the Nasdaq gained 0.5% to 27,068.72, and the Dow jumped 0.93% (478.64 points) to 51,828.62, snapping a three-week losing streak as all three indexes finished the week higher ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-september-25-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081738529.html)).
- **Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield finished Friday at 5.17% and the 2-year at 4.81%, while the 30-year touched 5.488%, its highest since 2004. The move was driven by hawkish comments from Fed Governor Michael Barr, elevated energy prices tied to the Iran war, and a hot PMI reading; futures markets now price roughly a 64% chance of an October Fed rate hike ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/25/treasury-yields-snapshot-september-25-2026)).
- **Housing**: Freddie Mac's weekly 30-year fixed average hit 7.03%, but daily trackers showed rates pushing as high as 7.2%–7.5% by Friday, up from 6.30% a year ago, as the housing market keeps losing buyers ([Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/current-mortgage-rates-09-25-2026/)). *Why it matters:* higher-for-longer yields are the transmission line from the Fed and oil-driven inflation fears into every mortgage payment.
- **Commodities**: Oil eased late in the week — WTI down 4.1% to $91.38, Brent to $97.45 — on signs of progress toward a U.S.-Iran deal on tanker access through the Gulf, while gold fell 2.1% on the week to $4,286.74 on rate-hike expectations and copper held near $6.70/lb, up 42% year-over-year ([ShareTrader](https://sharetrader.com.au/news/2026-09-26-copper-price-rises-as-gold-lithium-and-brent-crude-retreat-the-week-in-commodities); [TradingEconomics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold)). Chicago wheat settled at $7.03/bushel, October live cattle at $218.87 ([Brownfield Ag News](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/market-news/closing-grain-and-livestock-futures-september-25-2026/)).

## World

- **Japan**: The yen slid past 158/dollar to three-week lows before recovering slightly Friday after Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said President Trump had voiced concern about yen weakness — traders are watching the 160 level as a possible intervention trigger ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/yen-japan-intervention-boj.html)).
- **United Kingdom**: The Bank of England held its key rate at 3.75% on a 6-3 vote earlier this month as UK inflation runs at 3.1%, partly on Middle East-driven energy costs; the pound sits near $1.34, down about 2.5% over the past month ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026)).
- **China**: Xi Jinping wrapped a White House visit Thursday and Friday — his first U.S. trip since 2023 — with the two sides extending their trade truce to January, keeping tariffs and rare-earth export limits on hold; China also saw a surprise jump in U.S. orders ahead of the summit ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/china-exports-surplus-trade-tariffs-.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/trump-xi-summit-meet-white-house-tensions-taiwan-trade-ai-rcna598073)).

## Politics & Geopolitics

- **Iran & Red Sea**: Trump rejected Iran's proposal for a seven-day roadmap to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks, while Iranian President Pezeshkian told CBS News Tehran would let U.N. inspectors back in as part of a longer ceasefire deal ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/9/26/iran-war-live-tehran-offers-us-plan-to-reopen-hormuz-within-seven-days)). Iran-backed Houthi forces now control Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, pushing Saudi Arabia to reroute exports through Gulf-of-Oman ship transfers that are now at capacity ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/middle-east/houthis-control-bab-el-mandeb-yemen-red-sea-saudi-iran-war-oil-rcna597186)). *Why it matters:* squeezed shipping routes on both sides of the Arabian Peninsula keep a risk premium in oil, feeding this week's Treasury sell-off above.
- **AI policy**: Sens. Sanders and Rep. Casar introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, which would pause advanced AI development pending federal safety rules; California enacted a law requiring disclosure of AI-generated performers in ads ([Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/09/tech-bills-week-creating-ai-focused-agency-reviewing-ai-assisted-cyber-attacks-and-more/416253/?oref=ng-homepage-river)).

## Montana & Bozeman

- A new Montana Free Press–Rutgers Eagleton poll found 80% of Montana voters rank cost of living as very important to their midterm vote, well ahead of immigration, healthcare, and environmental policy ([Montana Free Press](https://montanafreepress.org/2026/09/24/cost-of-living-top-of-mind-montana-voters/)). *Why it matters for markets:* affordability pressure at the ballot box comes as Gallatin County's median home list price sits near $827,000 and national mortgage rates push toward 7.5% — the local housing squeeze and the national rate story described above are the same problem for Montana households.

If national mortgage rates keep climbing toward 7.5% while Gallatin County's median home price sits near $827,000, does easing come first from a Fed pivot, from local price cuts, or does the "frustrating equilibrium" just hold through the midterms?

Iran talks stalled over the weekend. Oil is up, gold is down, and Treasury yields are pushing toward levels last seen before the financial crisis.

## In 60 seconds

- Trump rejected Iran's Hormuz de-escalation offer; oil jumped and gold hit a seven-week low.
- The 10-year Treasury yield pushed to about 5.21%, near its highest since 2008, as October rate-hike odds hold near 70%.
- The U.S. and China released detailed goods lists Sunday for their $30-billion-for-$30-billion tariff-cut deal.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-09-28/markets.png)

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield rose to roughly 5.21% Monday, its highest since mid-2008, up from 5.18% Thursday ([TradingEconomics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield)). Futures price about a 66%-70% chance of an October Fed hike.

**Housing**: Freddie Mac's weekly 30-year average was 7.03% Thursday, but daily trackers show it climbing further into Monday, to about 7.36% ([Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/mortgages/mortgage-rates-09-28-26/)) — a third straight day of increases.

**Commodities**: Gold fell to about $4,189/oz Monday, down 1.1% from Friday and its lowest since August 5, as a stronger dollar and rising yields piled onto Iran-driven uncertainty ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-prices-today-monday-september-28-2026-gold-prices-slump-as-iran-tensions-and-oil-prices-rise-110730952.html)). Oil moved the other way: WTI jumped more than 4% intraday to a session peak near $96.44 a barrel after Trump rejected Iran's Hormuz proposal ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/us-stock-market-today-p-080713612.html)).

**Tariffs**: The U.S. and China released detailed lists Sunday for their tariff-cut deal from last week's Trump-Xi summit — 77 Chinese goods and 1,600-plus U.S. products proposed for lower duties ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/28/us-china-list-goods-recommended-for-tariff-cuts-following-trump-xi-summit)). Trade analyst Deborah Elms said the lists "do not move the needle on overall trade flows."

## World

**Japan**: The yen jumped as much as 0.4% to about 156.5/dollar, its strongest since September 18, after Japan's top currency official, Atsushi Mimura, said markets should take Tokyo's "very clear" warning on yen weakness at face value ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/japans-mimura-reiterates-fears-us-japan-joint-intervention-202609280900)).

**United Kingdom**: Five British men were arrested Sunday after bomb-disposal teams examined suspicious vehicles near RAF Fairford, a U.K. base used by American bombers; 85 nearby homes were evacuated. Investigators are examining possible links to Iran, Russia, or militant proxies ([MTPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2026-09-28/5-men-arrested-over-suspected-attack-at-a-u-s-run-air-force-base-are-british-nationals)).

## Politics & Geopolitics

**Iran & the Strait of Hormuz**: Trump told Axios he expects talks with Iran to resume this week despite rejecting Tehran's latest Hormuz proposal, saying "they want to make a deal, but it is not the deal that I want to make." Iran's foreign minister said Tehran was never formally notified of the rejection and remains open to diplomacy ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-trump-strait-of-hormuz-7-day-proposal/)). *Markets:* this is the direct driver of Monday's oil spike and gold selloff above.

**U.S. politics**: The White House expanded its taxpayer-funded ad campaign with a new spot featuring Trump warning of a "final battle" against the "fake news media" ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/09/27/white-house-expands-taxpayer-paid-ad-campaign-boosting-trump/)). *Markets:* no direct price impact, but it sharpens the partisan tone markets are pricing into midterm-outcome odds.

## One question

If Iran and the U.S. both say they're open to more talks, why does each new round of rejection keep landing as a fresh shock to oil and gold?

Stocks keep climbing even as Washington stays shut down, yields hold near two-decade highs, and Innodata gives back some of its recent AI-driven run.

## In 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 closed at 7,773.95 Monday, up 0.66% and a fresh record, while the Nasdaq rose 1.05% to 27,477.31.
- The federal government shutdown, now in its second week, continued after the Senate again failed Monday to reach 60 votes on either party's funding bill.
- Innodata fell 4.4% Monday to $66.97 as investors weighed its premium valuation against heavy customer concentration.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-06/markets.png)

**Stocks**: The S&P 500 rose 0.66% to a record 7,773.95 Monday, the Nasdaq jumped 1.05% to 27,477.31, and the Dow added 0.18% to 51,267.90. All three are up on the week, with the Nasdaq leading at +2.45%.

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.28%, up 11 bp on the week; the 2-year is 4.83% (+2 bp) and the 30-year 5.63% (+14 bp). Yields have climbed 11-49 bp this past month on heavy government debt supply, and a prolonged shutdown is starving the Fed of fresh labor and inflation data ahead of its October 29 meeting ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2025/10/markets-brush-off-government-shutdown-concerns-with-quick-resolution-expected-93420507)).

**Commodities**: WTI crude is $96.16/bbl, still up 13.7% on the month and near its highest level since the Iran war began.

## Watchlist

![Watchlist]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-06/watchlist.png)

**INOD** -4.42%: Innodata fell to $66.97 Monday, extending a pullback that has cut the stock nearly a third over three months. Analysts have trimmed 2026 EPS estimates to $1.01 from $1.12 in the past 60 days as the market reassesses how much AI-demand growth is already priced in and how reliant revenue is on a handful of big customers ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2900590/innodata-stock-plunges-328-in-3-months-time-to-hold-or-exit)).

Quiet: BB, JPM, GBCI.

## Politics & Geopolitics

**Shutdown stalemate**: The government shutdown that began October 1 is still running after the Senate failed Monday to advance either party's funding bill, with neither reaching the 60 votes needed. About 2 million federal workers are going without pay and roughly 750,000 are furloughed; troops and air traffic controllers are working unpaid. President Trump said he would discuss health-care subsidies with Democrats, but only after the government reopens. *Markets: equities have mostly shrugged off the standoff, but each week of shutdown shaves an estimated 0.1-0.2 percentage points off quarterly GDP, and the Fed is now making policy without its usual jobs and inflation data.* ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2025-10-09/senate-to-hold-7th-vote-as-government-shutdown-drags-on))

**Tariffs ahead**: New 10% tariffs on imported timber and lumber, plus 25% duties on kitchen cabinets, vanities, and upholstered furniture, take effect October 14; a 25% tariff on medium and heavy-duty trucks follows November 1. *Markets: the WTO raised its 2026 global trade-growth forecast to 2.4% from 0.9% on front-loaded shipping ahead of tariff deadlines, but cut its 2026 outlook as the tariffs bite over time.* ([MarketScreener](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/latest/Trump-s-trade-war-Tariff-deadlines-and-key-upcoming-events-50116473/))

## One question

Stocks keep setting records while the government stays shut and the Fed loses its usual data. Is the market right that the shutdown is just noise, or is it pricing in a resolution that hasn't happened yet?

A quiet weekend for news leaves markets picking up where Friday left off: yields near two-decade highs, stocks fresh off their best session in weeks, and JPMorgan opening the week with a dividend increase ahead of earnings.

## In 60 seconds

- JPMorgan raised its quarterly dividend 10%, to $1.65 a share from $1.50, with a record date of tomorrow, October 6.
- The S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow all closed Friday higher after the weak jobs report cooled bets on another Fed hike, though all three are still down or roughly flat on the week.
- Bank earnings season opens October 13, when JPMorgan reports Q3 results and sets the tone for the group.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-05/markets.png)

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.24%, the 2-year at 4.78% (down 9 bp on the week on Fed-hold bets), and the 30-year at 5.61% (up 14 bp on the week on fiscal-supply worries). The curve keeps steepening, and nothing over the weekend changed that story.

**Stocks**: The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,722.72, the Nasdaq at 27,190.86, and the Dow at 51,176.96, each up on the day as the soft jobs report cemented bets the Fed stays on hold this month. Markets are closed through the weekend with no fresh catalyst yet this morning.

**Commodities**: WTI crude is $96.16/bbl, flat on the day but still up 13.7% on the month, holding near its highest level since the Iran war began.

## One question

JPMorgan just raised its dividend heading into an earnings report where investors will be watching credit quality closely. Is a bigger payout a vote of confidence from the board, or just the easiest lever to pull when the stock itself has lagged the market this year?

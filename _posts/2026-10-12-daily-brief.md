Stocks closed out last week higher, BlackBerry snapped its slide Friday, and the week ahead brings bank earnings, CPI, and a deadline buried in the trade-policy fine print.

## In 60 seconds

- Bank earnings season opens Tuesday: JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo report before the open, with September CPI landing Wednesday.
- BlackBerry rose 3.24% Friday to $8.91, even as CEO John Giamatteo and three other executives sold shares October 7.
- China's one-year pause on expanded rare-earth export controls expires November 10, a deadline few are watching yet.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-12/markets.png)

**Stocks**: The S&P 500 rose 0.59% Friday to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq added 0.64% to 27,366.17, and the Dow gained 0.83% to 51,654.95. All three finished the week higher, led by the S&P's 1.15% gain.

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.22% (down 2 bp on the week) but is still up 42 bp on the month. The 2-year sits at 4.75% and the 30-year at 5.60%. September CPI lands Wednesday, ahead of the Fed's October 28-29 meeting.

**Housing**: The 30-year mortgage rate held at 7.40%, roughly a four-year high, unchanged from last week as the bond selloff keeps borrowing costs elevated.

**Commodities**: WTI crude is $96.24/bbl, flat on the week but up 3.83% on the month.

## World

**China's rare-earth truce has an expiration date.** Beijing paused expanded export controls on five rare-earth elements for one year after last October's trade talks with Washington; that pause lifts November 10. The separate April 2025 licensing regime on seven other rare earths (samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium, yttrium) stays in force regardless ([China Briefing](https://china-briefing.com/news/chinas-rare-earth-export-controls)).

## Montana & Bozeman

**Montana's marquee business summit lands in Bozeman this week.** Governor Gianforte and the Montana Chamber of Commerce co-host the Big Sky, Bright Future Economic Summit October 15 at The Commons, with this year's focus on tech and innovation, a sector that generated $7.9 billion in 2024 revenue according to the Montana High Tech Business Alliance ([Montana Chamber of Commerce](https://montanachamber.com/economic-summit/)).

Bank earnings and CPI both land this week while a one-year truce on Chinese rare-earth controls quietly expires in a month. Which of those three moves markets more?

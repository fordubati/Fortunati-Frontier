Wall Street's AI-stock anxiety widened into bank shares this week even after Nvidia beat earnings estimates, sending the Dow down 8.02% and every watchlist ticker into a double-digit decline. Treasury yields eased despite near-90% odds of a December Fed hike. The AP called Alaska's Senate race for Mary Peltola, Trump, Xi and Putin held an unprecedented three-way summit, and Montana's Attorney General said he won't defend a voter-approved corporate-spending ban.

## The week in 60 seconds

- Nvidia beat Q3 estimates Tuesday ($115.8 billion revenue, up 103% year over year) but AI-stock jitters spread into banks anyway: the S&P 500 fell 5.49% and the Dow 8.02% on the week even as Treasury yields eased.
- The AP called Alaska's Senate race for Democrat Mary Peltola, who closed a ranked-choice tabulation with 50.1% of first-choice votes; certification is set for Nov. 25, though a Trump DOJ appeal remains pending at the Ninth Circuit.
- Trump, Xi and Putin held their first-ever trilateral summit in Shenzhen Wednesday, producing a "peace declaration" that never mentions Ukraine, Iran, Taiwan or North Korea.
- Every watchlist ticker fell double digits this week: Innodata dropped another 20.7% (down 53.6% on the month), and Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan each fell more than 10% as AI-capex worries spread into banks.
- Montana's Attorney General said he won't defend the voter-approved "Montana Plan" corporate-spending ban in federal court, while Big Sky Resort confirmed its 2026-27 ski season opens Nov. 25.

### Bonds

**The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.04%, down 6 bp on the week, even as futures keep pricing roughly a 90% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike Dec. 8-9.** The 2-year fell further, down 10 bp to 4.76%, suggesting traders are weighing a slowing economy as much as sticky inflation. The 10-year/3-month spread is now about 70 bp, versus 18 bp a year ago, a sign the curve keeps normalizing after years near inversion. ## Questions to think about

- If Treasury yields fell the same week Fed-hike odds held near 90%, what does that say about which risk, inflation or growth, investors fear most right now?

## On the desk

**"Bank stocks fell double digits alongside AI-chip names. Should a treasury desk care?"** Falling equity prices don't directly raise funding costs, but if investors read the slide as cooling AI-driven loan and deposit growth, that view can tighten the wholesale funding markets banks rely on for liquidity.

**"The 10-year yield fell even as Fed-hike odds sit near 90%. What does that mean for funding costs?"** A falling long end against a rising short end flattens the curve banks price loans against, narrowing the spread a bank earns borrowing short and lending long.

**"Yields across the curve sit well above year-ago levels. What does that mean for a bank's bond portfolio?"** Bonds bought a year ago at lower yields are now worth less mark-to-market; holding them to maturity avoids booking that loss, but leaves less room to sell for liquidity without a capital hit.

Stocks keep grinding to new highs, the shutdown shows no sign of breaking, and the cost of a 30-year mortgage just hit its highest level in about four years.

## In 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,818.93 Tuesday, up 0.58%, its third straight record close.
- The government shutdown hit day 6; the Senate failed two more funding votes Tuesday and reconvenes Wednesday morning to try again.
- The 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 7.28%, up 25 basis points on the week and the highest in roughly four years.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-07/markets.png)

**Stocks**: The S&P 500 rose 0.58% to a record 7,818.93 Tuesday, the Nasdaq added 0.45% to 27,599.89, and the Dow gained 0.49% to 51,521.28. All three are higher on the week, led by the Nasdaq at +2.99%.

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield is 5.31%, up 3 bp on the day and 53 bp over the past month to near two-decade highs. The 2-year sits at 4.84% (down 8 bp on the week) and the 30-year at 5.66% (up 10 bp on the week). The shutdown is starving the Fed of jobs and inflation data ahead of its October 28-29 meeting.

**Housing**: The average 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 7.28%, up 25 bp this week and 62 bp over the month, its highest level in about four years, as the broader bond selloff pushes borrowing costs higher across the board ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/30-year-mortgage-rate/news/588887)).

**Commodities**: WTI crude is $96.16/bbl, still up 13.7% on the month.

## Watchlist

![Watchlist]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-07/watchlist.png)

**BB** +9.86% this week, to $9.58: BlackBerry extended its 2026 rally as investors keep buying into the QNX automotive-software and cybersecurity turnaround story; the stock is up roughly 150% over the past year even though fiscal 2026 revenue guidance ($504-534 million) still trails the $550 million analysts had expected ([Chartmill](https://www.chartmill.com/news/external/59941/is-bb-stock-still-worth-buying-after-its-massive-fiscal-2026-rally)).

Quiet: INOD, JPM, GBCI.

## World

**Japan**: Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda said the central bank wants more data on U.S. economic strength and Japanese wage growth before deciding on an October rate hike, pointing investor attention toward a possible move in December instead ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/japan/interest-rate/news/493836)).

## Politics & Geopolitics

**Shutdown, day 6**: The Senate failed two more funding votes Tuesday: Democrats' bill, which would extend expiring health-insurance tax credits, got 45; Republicans' bill, which would fund the government through November 21, got 52. Both needed 60. Roughly 2 million federal workers are going unpaid and 750,000 are furloughed. The Senate reconvenes Wednesday morning for another round. *Markets: stocks keep setting records through the standoff, but every week without jobs and inflation data leaves the Fed making its October decision with less information than usual.* ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/government-shutdown-latest-stalemate-trump-senate-vote/))

## One question

The S&P just notched its third straight record while Washington stays shut and mortgages hit a four-year high. If neither of those is denting stocks yet, what would it take?

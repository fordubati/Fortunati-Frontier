Wall Street is closed for Thanksgiving. The last trading day's news still matters: October core PCE inflation rose to its highest level in nearly three years, cementing bets on a December rate hike.

## In 60 seconds

- U.S. markets are closed for Thanksgiving; they reopen Friday for an abbreviated session that closes at 1 p.m. ET.
- October core PCE inflation rose to 3.3% year over year, the highest in nearly three years, with headline PCE at 3.8%; traders now put December rate-hike odds near 95%.
- The watchlist was quiet into the holiday, with no ticker moving close to 3%.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-11-26/markets.png)

**October core PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose to 3.3% year over year, up from 3.1% in September and above the 3.2% forecast.** Headline PCE hit 3.8%, up from 3.6%. The report, delayed by this year's government shutdown, is the last inflation read before the Dec. 8-9 Fed meeting. **Treasury yields fell this week despite the hotter inflation data: the 10-year is at 4.91%, down 19 bp, and the 2-year at 4.63%, down 17 bp.** The S&P 500 closed the week at 7,201, down 1.9%, and the Nasdaq at 23,586, down 3.46%, as this month's AI-stock swings continue. **The 30-year mortgage rate eased to 7.21% on Wednesday, down 7 bp on the day but still up 10 bp for the week.** WTI crude is at $82.24 a barrel, down 8.1% this week and 25% this month, a tailwind for the inflation outlook that hasn't yet shown up in the official data.

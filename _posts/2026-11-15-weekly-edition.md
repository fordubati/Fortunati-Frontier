Stocks and bond yields both jumped this week. A hot October CPI pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back above 5% and lifted December Fed-hike odds to roughly 91%, while a CoreWeave earnings beat sparked a rally across AI-cloud names that pulled the S&P 500 up 2.51%. Mississippi elected Democrat Scott Colom to the Senate, Alaska's race heads to a ranked-choice tabulation Nov. 18, and Bozeman dealt with a water-fee hike and an FAA ground stop that stranded the MSU football team.

## The week in 60 seconds

- A hotter-than-expected October CPI (core +0.3%) sent the 10-year Treasury yield back above 5% (5.10%, +26 bp on the week) and pushed December Fed-hike odds to about 91%, from roughly 21% a week earlier.
- The S&P 500 rose 2.51% and the Nasdaq 2.27% on the week as a CoreWeave earnings beat sparked a rally across AI-cloud stocks; Nvidia rose about 5% even as its board's probe into CEO Jensen Huang continued.
- Mississippi elected Democrat Scott Colom to the U.S. Senate, its first Black senator since Reconstruction and first Democratic senator since 1982; Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded Wednesday.
- Japan's Nikkei 225 rebounded 6.46% on the week to 61,142 as the Bank of Japan signaled it's leaning toward a December rate hike.
- Innodata fell another 4.24% to $35.46, now down 33% over the past month, with no new company news to explain the latest slide.

## Key Economies

### Japan

The Nikkei 225 rebounded 6.46% this week to close Friday at 61,142, recovering most of the prior week's AI-stock-rout losses. The Bank of Japan's Nov. 10 Summary of Opinions showed policymakers leaning toward a December hike from the current 1.25% rate. *Markets: a hawkish BOJ, alongside a hiking Bank of England and a Fed now leaning hawkish too, points to synchronized global tightening few expected a month ago.* [Read more →](https://tradingeconomics.com/japan/stock-market/news/599860)

### United Kingdom

The Bank of England's Nov. 4 Monetary Policy Report showed the UK economy running hotter than forecast: Q2 GDP grew 0.5% versus the 0.3% projected, with Q3 tracking 0.7%. The Bank estimates AI-related activity accounts for nearly a fifth of UK GDP growth since the start of 2026. *Markets: a hot UK economy narrows the case for rate cuts on either side of the Atlantic.* [Read more →](https://ppc.land/ai-accounts-for-nearly-a-fifth-of-uk-growth-in-2026-bank-of-england-finds/)

## Montana & Bozeman

### Housing

**Bozeman's city commission voted 5-0 Tuesday to raise its cash-in-lieu-of-water-rights fee from $6,000 to $13,050 per acre-foot**, a 117.5% jump lifting the typical new home's fee from about $1,400 to $3,000 a year. One commenter estimated the change would add over $1.5 million to a large project's costs; Commissioner Emma Bode said the fee hadn't moved in 18 years. [Read more →](https://www.explorebigsky.com/city-unanimously-oks-water-fee-hike/175628)

### Local Politics

**Bozeman residents marked a milestone for the "See Bozeman Creek" waterway vision plan Monday.** A 2025 engineering report found more than 20 undersized culverts and bridges along the creek, and found the Main Street tunnel can carry less than half the flow needed in a 100-year flood. The city has earmarked $150,000 for 2027 work; the full, 30-to-40-year vision goes to the city commission for adoption Dec. 15. [Read more →](https://www.explorebigsky.com/creek-peek-bozeman-waterway-vision-plan-celebrates-milestone)

**A nationwide FAA ground stop Friday hit Bozeman Yellowstone International Airport**, part of a broader outage Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed on an "overload of an old, analog system" linking regional air-traffic control centers. Montana State's football team sat on the tarmac roughly 30 minutes before its flight to Colorado was scrubbed, but still made Saturday's noon kickoff against Northern Colorado in Greeley. [Read more →](https://www.explorebigsky.com/nationwide-groundstop-affects-bozeman-yellowstone-international-airport/175675)

## Connecting the dots

- A hot CPI print pushed U.S. yields up alongside a hawkish Bank of Japan and an already-hiking Bank of England, a sign central banks are fighting the same sticky inflation even as AI-driven growth keeps surprising to the upside.
- Stocks rallied the same week bond yields spiked: equity investors cared more about CoreWeave's beat than about what a hotter Fed path means for valuations, at least for one week.
- Mississippi's flip and Bozeman's two infrastructure bills share a theme: long-deferred costs, an 18-year-frozen water fee or a Senate seat Republicans hadn't seriously defended in decades, are coming due at once.

## Questions to think about

- If one hot CPI print can swing Fed-hike odds from 21% "no change" to 91% "hike" in a week, how much should investors trust any single month of inflation data?
- AI-cloud stocks rallied hard on CoreWeave's beat just one week after a rout driven by AI-bubble fears. Is that volatility noise around a real trend, or a market that still doesn't know what AI capex is worth?
- Mississippi elected a Democrat in a state rated safely Republican. Is that a sign of a national mood, or two candidates who simply outperformed their parties?

## Interview angle

**"Why did stocks and bond yields both rise in the same week?"** Hot inflation usually hurts both; this week equity investors weighted CoreWeave's earnings beat more heavily than the Fed's hawkish shift, since strong AI-cloud demand supports earnings even as higher rates raise the discount on future profits.

**"What's actually pushing the Fed toward a December hike instead of a pause?"** October's CPI came in hotter than forecast on both headline and core measures, and Fed Governor Waller had already flagged that a core reading at or above 0.3% would put a bigger move "on the table," so markets repriced the odds accordingly.

**"Why does a Bank of Japan decision matter to a U.S. investor?"** Investors often borrow cheap yen to buy higher-yielding assets elsewhere (the "carry trade"); a BOJ hike that strengthens the yen can force them to unwind those trades, pulling money out of markets like U.S. stocks and bonds.

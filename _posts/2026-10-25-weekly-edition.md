A Meta-led AI-capex scare sent the 10-year Treasury yield down 19 bp in a day, its sharpest drop since 2023, while chip stocks whipsawed and Glacier Bancorp posted a record quarter. Oil held near $108 on Hormuz risk and a reportedly delayed Israeli strike on Iran. The Fed meets this week with no clear signal either way.

## The week in 60 seconds

- The 10-year Treasury yield fell 19 bp Thursday, its sharpest one-day drop since March 2023, on an AI-stocks-to-safety rotation; it sits at 4.96%, down 14 bp on the week.
- Meta fell nearly 7% Thursday on its plan to issue non-voting stock to fund AI spending, dragging the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 6.7% and the Nasdaq 2.6% that day.
- The watchlist had its best week in a month: INOD +12.44%, BB +10.67%, and GBCI +9.08% on a record quarter, while JPM slipped 2.40%.
- September housing starts fell 5.2% to 1.221 million, even as the 30-year mortgage rate eased 8 bp to 7.32%, its first pullback in weeks.
- Israel had an Iran bombing campaign ready for October 10 that Trump delayed until after the midterms, the Financial Times reports; oil is still up 13.1% on the week.

### Bonds

**Thursday's 19 bp yield plunge was the biggest one-day drop since the Silicon Valley Bank scare of March 2023.** Investors fled AI-exposed stocks for Treasuries after Meta's spending plans spooked the market; CoreWeave fell another 10% as credit spreads widened. The move came despite strong jobless-claims data that would normally push yields up, not down. [Read more →](https://finance.biggo.com/news/8ab817e1-258c-47c0-8bad-6f17b6bfe3df)

**Futures now price just a 7% chance of an October hike, versus 76-78% for December.** The September PCE report, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, lands Thursday, right after the decision. [Read more →](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/23/the-10-year-yield-just-fell-below-5-as-investors-fled-ai-stocks/)

### Stocks

**AI-capex anxiety, not Fed policy, drove the week.** Meta dropped close to 7% Thursday over its plan to issue non-voting stock to help cover AI spending already guided at $130-145 billion for 2026; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had its worst day since June, down 6.7%, and the Nasdaq fell 2.6% that session, closing the week down 0.71%. The Dow fell further Friday, off 1.23% to 52,215.16; the S&P held up best, up 0.50%. [Read more →](https://finance.biggo.com/news/8ab817e1-258c-47c0-8bad-6f17b6bfe3df)

### Housing

**September housing starts fell 5.2% from August to a 1.221 million annual rate, down 7.4% year-over-year.** Permits held closer to flat at 1.400 million. The 30-year mortgage rate eased 8 bp to 7.32%, its first decline after an eight-week climb. [Read more →](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/pdf/newresconst_202609.pdf)

### Tariffs & Trade

**US and Chinese negotiators are racing to lock in a deal before November's APEC summit in Shenzhen.** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent may skip APEC's Hong Kong finance-minister meeting to focus on Shenzhen prep instead, meeting Vice-Premier He Lifeng first. China's separate rare-earth export-control suspension still expires November 10. *Markets: a deal before Shenzhen would remove a lingering tail risk for chip and auto names.* [Read more →](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3370295/bessent-may-skip-apec-hong-kong-focus-trumps-shenzhen-visit-sources-say)

## Key Economies

### Japan

The Bank of Japan meets Thursday and Friday, its first Outlook Report since September's hike to 1.25%, a 31-year high. Markets see another hike as unlikely, near one-in-four odds. *Markets: a dovish hold widens the gap with the Fed and pressures the yen.* [Read more →](https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-s-ueda-in-focus-as-markets-weigh-a-back-to-back-october-rate-hike/)

### United Kingdom

UK inflation ran 3.1% year-over-year in August, more than a point above the Bank of England's 2% target, while September retail sales beat forecasts, up 1.0%. The BOE holds at 3.75%. *Markets: inflation well above target leaves little room for a near-term cut even with spending holding up.* [Read more →](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/brc-retail-sales-monitor-yoy/news/591253)

### China

China's official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.1 in September, back into expansion on a seasonal restocking wave, though the employment sub-index slipped to 48.4, a sign of soft domestic demand. *Markets: an export-led bounce, not a domestic-demand one, keeps pressure on Beijing to protect its rare-earth leverage in the Shenzhen talks.* [Read more →](https://datatrack.trendforce.com/blog/content/64764/chinas-manufacturing-pmi-returns-to-expansion-zone-latest-q3-2026-data-rises-to-501)

## Politics & Geopolitics

### Conflicts

**Israel had an Iran bombing campaign ready for the weekend of October 10, which Trump delayed until after the midterms, the Financial Times reports.** Sources say the planned strikes, targeting nuclear, regime, and energy sites, were more intense than February's joint US-Israeli operation. Trump said the US would not strike before November 3, then hedged: "we'll see." *Markets: a strike delayed, not called off, keeps a standing risk premium under oil.* [Read more →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-planned-attack-on-iran-earlier-this-month-but-trump-opposed-it-report/)

**Hormuz tanker attacks continued for a third straight week**, with two more vessels hit off Oman and Iraq; October's count tops 28, though the pace has slowed. [Read more →](https://seatrade-maritime.com/security/fire-on-kotc-vessel-struck-off-oman-tanker-hit-near-iraq)

## Connecting the dots

- The same AI-capex worry that hit Meta also pulled Treasury yields down 19 bp in a day, showing how intertwined the "AI trade" and the bond market have become.
- Oil's risk premium now stacks two triggers: ongoing Hormuz attacks and a delayed, not cancelled, Israeli strike plan on Iran that could reignite after November 3.
- Falling yields should eventually help housing, but September's starts (down 5.2%) reflect rate-lock decisions from a month ago; watch whether October's numbers show this week's mortgage dip.

## Questions to think about

- If one company's capex plan can move the Treasury market 19 bp in a day, how much of the "higher for longer" rate story is about inflation versus AI-spending sentiment?
- Israel's strike on Iran is delayed, not cancelled, until after two elections. How should markets price a risk scheduled to resolve by a date, rather than at random?
- JPMorgan fell when yields fell, but Glacier Bancorp posted a record quarter. When does a falling-rate environment start separating winners from losers within the same sector?

## Interview angle

**"Why did Treasury yields have their biggest one-day drop in years this week?"** Meta's plan to fund AI spending with new non-voting stock renewed capex worries, and investors rotated from tech into Treasuries, pushing the 10-year down 19 bp in a session.

**"What's the market pricing for the Fed this month?"** Futures put October hike odds near 7%, effectively ruling one out, versus 76-78% for December; Thursday's PCE report is the next number that could move that.

**"How are the midterms and Iran connected?"** Israel reportedly had a strike ready for October 10 that Trump delayed to avoid disrupting the vote, meaning the election itself could trigger what happens next in the Gulf.

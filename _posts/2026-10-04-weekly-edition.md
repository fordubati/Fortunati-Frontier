The week's through-line was relief at the margin, not a reversal. Friday's jobs report showed just 29,000 positions added against an 84,000 forecast, unemployment ticking up to 4.2%, and markets cheered the cooler odds of another Fed hike. But relief only went so far: the 30-year mortgage held near 7.28%, long Treasury yields stayed near their highest since the early 2000s, and JPMorgan was the one watchlist name in the red. In Washington, the Senate blocked a bill to protect ratepayers from AI data-center costs for the second time, a fight with a direct echo in Montana, where the state also began enforcing new Medicaid work rules on 71,000 residents this week. Here's the full picture.

## The week in 60 seconds

- September payrolls rose just 29,000 versus 84,000 expected; unemployment ticked up to 4.2%, and markets cheered the drag on Fed-hike odds.
- The 30-year fixed mortgage rate hit 7.28%, up 25 bp on the week and near a three-year high.
- The Senate blocked the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act for a second time, 57-43, over a dispute on whether data centers must cover their own grid costs.
- BlackBerry was the week's best watchlist performer, up 13.3%, while JPMorgan was the lone decliner, down 3.1%.
- Montana began enforcing Medicaid work requirements on October 1, affecting roughly 71,000 residents amid a rocky rollout.

### Bonds

The 10-year Treasury yield ended the week at 5.24%, up 6 bp, after climbing as high as 5.29% Thursday; the 30-year hit 5.61%, up 14 bp and still near its highest since the early 2000s ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/treasury-yields-bonds-selloff.html)). The 2-year fell 9 bp to 4.78% as Friday's weak jobs report cooled bets on another October hike and yields "tumbled" into the close ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-october-2-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-september-jobs-report-080623878.html)). *Why it matters:* the curve is bull-steepening — short rates fall on Fed-hold bets, long rates stay elevated on fiscal worries — visible in this week's yield-curve chart against a year ago, when the front end was inverted.

### Stocks

Equities finished mixed: the Nasdaq eked out a 0.45% weekly gain, the S&P 500 slipped 0.27%, and the Dow lagged with a 1.26% drop. Friday was the week's best session — the S&P rose 0.73%, the Nasdaq 1.19%, the Dow 0.49% — after the soft jobs report cemented bets the Fed stays on hold, with Nvidia pushing the Nasdaq to an intraday record Thursday ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/01/stock-market-today-live-updates-.html)).

### Housing

The 30-year fixed mortgage rate climbed to 7.28%, up 25 bp on the week and 62 bp on the month, near its highest level in three years as the broader bond selloff kept borrowing costs elevated ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/current-mortgage-rates-10-01-2026/)). *Why it matters:* this is the same yield story from Bonds landing directly on household budgets, and on Bozeman's own cooling market below.

### Tariffs & Trade

A three-judge Court of International Trade panel heard arguments this week on the Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs covering 60 trading partners and 99.4% of U.S. imports, in a suit arguing the rates were set before the underlying investigation concluded ([Agri-Pulse](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/25322-daybreak-sept-30-tariff-case-to-be-argued-in-itc)). The tariffs replaced the IEEPA tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February. No ruling yet. *Why it matters:* a loss would unwind duties covering nearly all U.S. trade, adding uncertainty just as the economy slows.

### Commodities

WTI crude finished flat (-0.3%) at $96.16/bbl but still up 13.7% on the month, holding near its highest since the Iran war began. Gold was choppy, sliding to a seven-week low near $4,150/oz Tuesday before rebounding 1.4% to about $4,185 Wednesday as a softer Fed tone offset oil's moves ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-price-today-tuesday-september-29-2026-gold-opens-near-2-month-low-but-brent-crude-also-moving-lower-104439751.html)).

## Key Economies

### Japan

The yen fell to about 158.21/dollar Wednesday, the worst G-10 performer, after a Bank of Japan summary disappointed investors hoping for clearer signals on hikes beyond September's move to 1.25% ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9104635/japanese-yen-weakens-as-bojs-meeting-summary-disappoints-investors)). The Nikkei jumped 3.3% Thursday to 68,956.72 on the same tech rally lifting Wall Street. *Markets:* a hawkish BOJ still can't strengthen the yen against U.S. yields this high.

### United Kingdom

The 10-year gilt yield eased to 5.38% Friday, just off its highest since July 2007, as investors paused after weeks of selling tied to fiscal concerns; sterling held near $1.324 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/government-bond-yield)). *Markets:* UK borrowing costs are rising for the same reason as the U.S.'s — persistent inflation and heavy bond supply.

### China

The official factory PMI returned to expansion at 50.1 in September, up from 49.8, snapping a two-month contraction, helped by new mortgage-interest subsidies and stronger high-tech output ([AP via WSLS](https://www.wsls.com/business/2026/09/30/factory-activity-returns-to-expansion-in-a-positive-sign-for-chinas-economy/)). The Trump-Xi tariff truce continues to hold. *Markets:* a China stabilizing on its own stimulus has less reason to make further trade concessions.

## Politics & Geopolitics

### Conflicts

The U.S. completed its full troop withdrawal from Iraq on September 30, ending a 23-year presence, as Baghdad declared a two-day "sovereignty day" holiday ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/30/nx-s1-5983426/u-s-withdraws-final-troops-from-iraq)). The Iran standoff over the Strait of Hormuz ground on with no deal, even as Middle East crude exports rebounded to 12.8 million barrels a day — the highest since the war began — as Saudi Arabia rerouted flows around the strait ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/09/30/why-rebound-oil-shipments-isnt-bringing-big-drop-prices/)). *Markets:* rerouted supply, not an actual reopening, is why oil's risk premium held even as prices stayed flat.

### U.S. National Politics

Trump's approval slid further: a Strength In Numbers/Verasight poll put it at a term-low 33% on September 23, while the broader polling average sat near 44% approve, 54% disapprove by month's end ([uspollingdata.com](https://uspollingdata.com/news/trump-approval-new-low-september-2026/)). *Markets:* a softening approval rating ahead of the midterms keeps divided-government odds elevated, historically lower-volatility for fiscal policy either way.

### Global Affairs

The Senate blocked the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act for a second time on September 30, falling short 57-43 of the 60 votes needed. Minority Leader Schumer called the voluntary bill "toothless" for not making data-center cost-sharing mandatory ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/09/30/voluntary-ratepayer-protection-bill-blocked-in-senate-again/)). *Markets:* the same cost question — who pays for the power AI data centers consume — is playing out at the county level in Montana below.

## Montana & Bozeman

### Housing

Bozeman's average home value sits at $725,769 through August, up just 0.4% year over year, with homes going to pending in about 24 days and 513 active listings ([Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/bozeman-mt/home-values/)). That's continued slow cooling from 2023's roughly $898,000 peak, colliding with mortgage rates near three-year highs nationally (see Housing above).

### Business

Joe's Parkway Market, the family-run Bozeman grocery near MSU, is proceeding toward its planned early-October closing, with the owners again citing the strain of running a small, aging building as the city grows ([KBZK](https://www.kbzk.com/news/joes-parkway-to-close-after-57-years-in-bozeman)).

### Local Politics

Montana began enforcing new Medicaid work requirements on October 1, affecting nearly 71,000 enrollees who must document 80 hours a month of work, volunteering, or study, or qualify for an exemption ([KUNC/NPR](https://www.kunc.org/npr-news/2026-09-30/montana-starts-enforcing-medicaid-work-requirements-on-oct-1-patients-are-confused)). The rollout has been rocky: confusing letters, a help-line with long waits, and an assistance nonprofit that cut staff from 18 to two after losing federal funding. Senate President Matt Regier said the deadline gave the state "more than enough time to work out the kinks." Butte-Silver Bow's data-center ballot fight, meanwhile, remains unresolved at the Montana Supreme Court heading into November.

## Connecting the dots

- Friday's jobs miss (Bonds, Stocks) cooled Fed-hike odds at the short end, but the long end — and the mortgage rate it feeds (Housing) — barely moved, the same split showing up in UK gilts (United Kingdom) for similar fiscal reasons.
- The Senate's second rejection of the Ratepayer Protection Act (Global Affairs) leaves the exact question open in Butte-Silver Bow (Local Politics): who pays when an AI data center shows up and the power bill follows.
- Rerouted, not resolved, is this week's pattern twice: oil exports rerouting around a still-closed Strait of Hormuz (Conflicts), and markets rerouting around a Fed that's paused, not retreated (Bonds).

## Questions to think about

- If long-term yields stay elevated even as short-term hike odds fade, is the bond market pricing a different risk than the Fed — and which one wins?
- Montana's Medicaid rollout lost most of its outside assistance capacity just as new paperwork requirements began. How much of the expected coverage loss is really about eligibility, versus administrative friction?
- The Ratepayer Protection Act failed twice on a "voluntary versus mandatory" divide. Is a mandatory federal cost-allocation rule even enforceable when electricity rates are set state by state?

## Interview angle

**Q: A weak jobs report usually spooks markets. Why did stocks rally instead?**
A: The market had been bracing for a hawkish Fed, and 29,000 jobs against an 84,000 forecast took that fear off the table. Traders concluded the Fed has less reason to hike this month, so short-term yields fell and rate-sensitive tech caught a bid.

**Q: How should a Montana reader think about a fight like the Ratepayer Protection Act?**
A: It's the same question Butte-Silver Bow is fighting locally, at a different altitude: when a data center needs new power infrastructure, does the company pay, or does it land on everyone's electric bill? Congress couldn't agree on a mandatory answer this week, so that fight stays with states and counties for now.

**Q: Mortgage rates near 7.3% and a weak jobs report both happened this week. Are they connected?**
A: Yes, through the same bond market. Mortgage rates track the 10-year Treasury yield, not the Fed funds rate directly, so even a report that cools Fed-hike odds doesn't immediately bring mortgage rates down if long-term yields stay elevated for other reasons, like this week's fiscal worries.

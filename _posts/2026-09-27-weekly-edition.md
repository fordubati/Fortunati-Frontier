The week's through-line was a widening gap between Wall Street and Main Street: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq climbed to fresh highs on a narrow rally led by AI winners like Meta, even as the bond market sold off hard enough to push the 30-year Treasury yield to its highest level since 2004 and the 30-year mortgage rate above 7% for the first time since January 2025. In Washington, the Trump-Xi summit extended the U.S.-China trade truce and even trimmed some tariffs, while a Court of International Trade hearing next week could upend the legal basis for the rest of them. In Bozeman's backyard, Butte-Silver Bow's data-center ballot fight put a local face on the same AI-infrastructure boom that's driving the stock market's gains. Here's the full picture.

## Markets

### Bonds

Treasury yields ended the week almost flat on the 10-year — 5.17% Friday versus roughly 5.16% the prior Friday — but that headline hides a wild ride: the note touched 5.22% intraweek, a level last seen in 2007, before easing back ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/25/treasury-yields-snapshot-september-25-2026)). The real story was the long end: the 30-year bond hit 5.488%–5.501%, its highest since 2004, while the 2-year sat at 4.81% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/us-treasury-yields-bonds-fed-inflation.html); [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/25/treasury-yields-snapshot-september-25-2026)). The selling was driven by hawkish comments from Fed Governor Michael Barr, a hot PMI print, and an oil market still pricing Middle East risk; futures now price roughly a 64% chance of another Fed hike in October, up sharply from recent weeks ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/25/treasury-yields-snapshot-september-25-2026)). *Why it matters for markets:* a hiking Fed is the direct transmission line into this week's mortgage-rate news below.

Credit markets aren't flinching, though. The ICE BofA high-yield spread sat near 2.80% (266–281 bps) — the tightest decile of its history versus a long-run median near 450 bps — while investment-grade spreads held near 0.79% over Treasuries ([Convex](https://convextrade.com/metrics/bamlh0a0hym2)). *Why it matters:* tight spreads say bond investors are pricing a higher rate path, not a recession — a very different signal than this yield move would send if spreads were also blowing out.

### Stocks

Equities notched a winning week even as bonds sold off, but a narrow one. The S&P 500 rose 1.21% to 7,743.41, the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.06% to 27,068.72, the Dow added a modest 0.28% to 51,828.62 (snapping a three-week losing streak), and the small-cap Russell 2000 actually slipped 0.80% to 2,837.55 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-september-25-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081738529.html)). That gap is the tell: money crowded into large caps seen as able to shrug off higher borrowing costs, while more than 70% of individual stocks fell on at least one mid-week session.

Meta was the standout, surging roughly 13–16% on the launch of its "Muse" AI agent — a move that also lit a fire under smaller AI-services names (see Watchlist below) ([Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/innodata-inod-stock-surges-15-on-speculation-of-meta-muse-partnership)). Nike was downgraded to Underperform by Bank of America and remains down sharply for the year, and bank stocks fell about 2.6% in a single session on worry that AI agents could disrupt deposit flows. Costco beat earnings expectations with revenue of $95.72 billion (versus $94.88 billion expected) and EPS of $6.75 (versus $6.53), citing continued consumer resilience ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-september-25-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081738529.html)).

### Housing

The 30-year fixed mortgage averaged 7.03% in Freddie Mac's weekly survey (up from 6.95%), the first reading above 7% since January 2025 and up from 6.30% a year ago — a fifth straight weekly increase ([Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms); [AgriNews](https://www.agrinews-pubs.com/business/2026/09/26/mortgage-rates-climb-for-5th-straight-week/)). Daily trackers ran hotter into the weekend, with Zillow's rate at 7.30% by Saturday ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/mortgages/article/mortgage-and-refinance-interest-rates-today-saturday-september-26-2026-mortgage-rates-keep-rising-into-the-weekend-100000421.html)). Existing-home sales fell 2.0% in August though remain up 1.6% year-to-date, and housing starts ran at a seasonally adjusted 1.275 million annual rate ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/current/index.html)). *Why it matters:* this is the same Fed-driven yield story from Bonds landing on household budgets — see Montana & Bozeman below.

### Tariffs & Trade

The headline was cooperative: President Trump hosted Xi Jinping at the White House, and the U.S. and China agreed to cut tariffs on $30 billion of goods — covering U.S. exports like agricultural products, wood, and cosmetics, and Chinese imports like small appliances and toys — while extending their trade truce to January and launching an AI dialogue ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-09-26/china-us-agree-to-30-billion-tariff-cut-ai-dialogue-during-xi-visit)), building on [Saturday's Daily Brief](https://fordubati.github.io/Fortunati-Frontier/2026/09/26/daily-brief/). China's trade engine is roaring into the talks: August exports rose 25% year-over-year, hitting a weekly record just before the summit ([Vantage Markets](https://www.vantagemarkets.com/market-news/china-trade-volumes-record-trump-xi-summit-september-24-2026/)).

The legal picture is less settled. On September 30, the Court of International Trade hears a challenge to Trump's tariff approach, with Alan Wolff — who helped draft the Section 301 provision the administration is now leaning on — arguing it goes beyond what Congress authorized ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/25/economy/tariffs-trump-section-301-case)). That follows a February Supreme Court ruling barring tariffs under the IEEPA and a separate ruling against a 10% blanket tariff as "unauthorized by law." *Why it matters for markets:* a ruling that unwinds the legal basis entirely could force sudden supply-chain re-pricing — worth watching regardless of the détente with China.

### Commodities

Oil eased for the week — WTI down roughly 4% to $91.38 and Brent to $97.45 — as U.S. and Iranian negotiators explored a phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, even as the broader war showed no sign of resolution (see Politics & Geopolitics below) ([ShareTrader](https://sharetrader.com.au/news/2026-09-26-copper-price-rises-as-gold-lithium-and-brent-crude-retreat-the-week-in-commodities)). Gold fell about 2% to roughly $4,280–4,287/oz as a two-month-high dollar and rising yields competed with safe-haven demand ([USAGOLD](https://www.usagold.com/daily-precious-metals-market-report-september-25-2026/)). Copper held up better, firming to around $6.70/lb (+42% y/y) on tight Chinese supply before a stronger dollar pared gains Friday ([ShareTrader](https://sharetrader.com.au/news/2026-09-26-copper-price-rises-as-gold-lithium-and-brent-crude-retreat-the-week-in-commodities); [TradingEconomics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/copper)). For Montana readers: Chicago wheat settled at $7.03/bushel, October live cattle at $218.87 ([Brownfield Ag News](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/market-news/closing-grain-and-livestock-futures-september-25-2026/)).

## Key Economies

### Japan

The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate a quarter point to 1.25% on September 18 — a 31-year high for Japanese borrowing costs — in a split 7-2 vote, citing the risk that inflation overshoots its 2% target ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/japan-raises-rates-30-year-high-yen-jgb.html)). Counterintuitively, the yen sold off on the hike, with dollar-yen rallying back past 156 and drifting toward 158 later in the week before recovering slightly Friday after Japan's finance minister said President Trump had voiced concern about yen weakness — traders are watching the 160 level as a possible intervention trigger ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/yen-japan-intervention-boj.html)). *Why it matters for markets:* a hawkish BOJ narrowing the U.S.-Japan rate gap would normally strengthen the yen, so its failure to do so this week says currency markets are more focused on the relentless rise in U.S. yields described above.

### United Kingdom

The Bank of England held its key rate at 3.75% on a 6-3 vote at its September meeting, with the three dissenters wanting a hike to 4% ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026)). UK inflation rose to 3.1% in August, and the Bank expects it to reach "slightly above 4%" by early 2027 — with Middle East-driven energy costs an explicit contributor, tying directly back to the conflict coverage below. The pound weakened to around $1.32, its lowest in three months, as a stronger dollar reflected growing U.S. rate-hike odds. *Why it matters for markets:* a central bank fighting energy-driven inflation it can't control with rate hikes alone is a preview of the bind other economies could face if Middle East risk premiums persist.

### China

Beyond the Trump-Xi summit and tariff cuts covered above, China's broader strategy has been building self-sufficiency to insulate its domestic market from trade swings, even as its export engine — up 25% year-over-year in August — keeps running hot ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/23/trump-xi-meeting-why-chinas-self-sufficiency-changes-the-calculus.html)). *Why it matters for markets:* a China that's less dependent on U.S. demand has more room to hold firm in future trade negotiations, which is part of why this week's truce extension reads as a mutual pause rather than a resolution.

## Politics & Geopolitics

### Conflicts

The Iran war and the shipping crisis it triggered ground on with no resolution. Trump rejected an Iranian proposal for a seven-day roadmap to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, even as Iran's president told CBS News Tehran would let U.N. inspectors back in as part of a longer ceasefire ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/9/26/iran-war-live-tehran-offers-us-plan-to-reopen-hormuz-within-seven-days)) — the same negotiations that helped ease oil prices this week (see Commodities above). An Iranian commercial vessel was struck near the Strait early Sunday, killing one person. Meanwhile, Houthi forces cemented control of Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline after a weeklong offensive, forcing Saudi Arabia to reroute oil exports through an already-strained Gulf-of-Oman transfer system, while Houthi strikes on Saudi cities including Riyadh and Taif continued ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/middle-east/houthis-control-bab-el-mandeb-yemen-red-sea-saudi-iran-war-oil-rcna597186)). *Why it matters for markets:* with both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb contested, oil's risk premium now has two chokepoints to worry about — a key reason gold and Treasuries stayed bid despite hints of a diplomatic off-ramp.

### U.S. National Politics

Democrats now hold a 9-point lead over Republicans on the generic congressional ballot (52%-43%), their largest of the 2026 cycle, as Trump's approval rating has slipped to 39% in NBC News's polling average, with even Republican approval softening slightly to 79% ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/nbc-news-polling-averages-democrats-enjoy-biggest-lead-yet-race-congre-rcna599378)). The Supreme Court for the third time blocked Missouri Republicans from using a new congressional map in the already-underway midterms. Separately, the White House said Friday it plans to cut $810 million in previously bipartisan, congressionally approved funding affecting immigrant, minority, and HHS research programs, drawing criticism from both parties. *Why it matters for markets:* a widening generic-ballot gap raises the odds of divided government after November — historically less legislative and fiscal-stimulus risk for markets to price either way.

### Global Affairs

Congress returned to a familiar deadlock on AI regulation even as concern grows. House Speaker Mike Johnson said AI "is serious" but called for industry self-regulation rather than new federal rules, while Senators Jon Husted and Martin Heinrich pushed the Ratepayer Protection Act to stop everyday electricity customers from subsidizing AI data-center power costs ([Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/09/tech-bills-week-creating-ai-focused-agency-reviewing-ai-assisted-cyber-attacks-and-more/416253/)). California Governor Gavin Newsom, meanwhile, issued an executive order accelerating the state's new independent AI oversight law and an AI "kill switch" requirement. *Why it matters for markets:* the ratepayer question is directly relevant to Montana, where data-center power demand just became a live local ballot issue — see below.

## Montana & Bozeman

### Housing

Bozeman's housing market kept cooling from its pandemic-era highs: the average home value sits at $733,959, down 0.9% over the past year, with homes going to pending in around 20 days and about 288 active listings on the market as of September ([Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/home-values/44281/bozeman-mt/)). That's a genuinely different market than 2023's peak (a median around $898,000 that spring), but it's still colliding with the 7%-plus mortgage rates described above — a national rate story and a local affordability story that are, as we noted in [Saturday's Daily Brief](https://fordubati.github.io/Fortunati-Frontier/2026/09/26/daily-brief/), the same underlying problem.

### Business

Glacier Bancorp's dividend increase (see Watchlist above) is a sign of confidence from one of Montana's largest banks even as the rate environment gets tougher for lenders generally. D.A. Davidson, the Great Falls-founded investment bank, kept up a steady drumbeat of deal activity, including serving as exclusive financial advisor on a debt refinancing for Day Wireless Systems and hosting its 25th annual industrials conference in Nashville. On Main Street, the closure of Joe's Parkway Market became a small but telling data point on Bozeman's growth pressures, with the owner telling the Chronicle "it's just hard to sustain a small business" even as the city keeps expanding ([Bozeman Daily Chronicle](https://www.bozemandailychronicle.com/news/)).

### Local Politics

The week's most consequential Montana story may be out of Butte, not Bozeman: the Montana Supreme Court first ruled that Butte-Silver Bow voters could decide a ballot initiative requiring two-thirds voter approval before any new data center could be built in the county, only to reverse itself days later and vacate that order after learning ballots had already been printed and mailed without the measure ([Daily Montanan](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/09/25/montana-supreme-court-vacates-decision-to-put-butte-data-center-issue-to-voters/); [Montana Free Press](https://montanafreepress.org/2026/09/23/supreme-court-puts-data-center-initiative-back-on-ballot/)). *Why it matters for markets:* this is a local preview of the exact tension in this week's Global Affairs item above — who pays for the power AI data centers consume, and who gets a say in where they're built. Separately, a Montana Free Press-Rutgers Eagleton poll found only 36% of Montanans think the state is headed in the right direction, with 80% ranking cost of living as very important to their midterm vote — well ahead of immigration or healthcare ([Montana Free Press](https://montanafreepress.org/2026/09/22/montana-voters-disapprove-state-direction-and-officials/)).

## Connecting the Dots

- The Fed's hawkish tilt (Bonds) is the common thread running through this week's mortgage-rate news (Housing), the bank-stock wobble on AI-disruption fears (Stocks), and even the yen's failure to strengthen after a BOJ hike (Japan) — U.S. yields are currently the dominant force in global markets.
- Two separate Middle East chokepoints — the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb (Conflicts) — are keeping a floor under oil's risk premium even as the headline price eased this week (Commodities), and that same energy-cost pressure is pushing UK inflation higher (United Kingdom).
- The AI-agent theme that lifted Meta and Innodata (Stocks, Watchlist) is the same force behind the federal ratepayer-cost debate (Global Affairs) and Butte-Silver Bow's data-center ballot fight (Local Politics) — one story, told at three different altitudes.
- The Trump-Xi trade truce (Tariffs & Trade, China) bought a few months of calm just as the legal foundation for the rest of Trump's tariffs heads to court on September 30 — the diplomatic and judicial tracks are running on very different timelines.
- Montana's cost-of-living anxiety (Local Politics) and the national generic-ballot shift toward Democrats (U.S. National Politics) both trace back to the same affordability pressure showing up in this week's mortgage and housing data.

## Questions to Think About

- If the Fed keeps hiking into a bond market that's already pricing multi-decade-high long yields, at what point does that start to look like a policy mistake rather than inflation-fighting — and who decides?
- Tight credit spreads suggest bond investors see no recession risk despite the yield spike. What would have to change for that view to flip, and how quickly could spreads reprice if it did?
- If AI agents really can disrupt bank deposit flows, as this week's bank-stock selloff suggests investors fear, what would that mean for how Main Street banks like Glacier Bancorp compete with money-market alternatives?
- Butte-Silver Bow's voters may get a direct say over data-center construction. If more Montana counties followed suit, would that meaningfully slow AI infrastructure buildout, or would developers simply build in counties that don't ask?
- The Trump-Xi summit extended a trade truce without resolving the underlying disputes. Is a series of extensions functionally the same as a permanent deal, or does the uncertainty itself carry a cost that compounds each time it's renewed?

## Interview Angle

**Q: Long-term Treasury yields just hit their highest levels since 2004. Walk me through why that matters beyond the bond market itself.**
A: Long yields are the base rate for a huge range of borrowing costs — mortgages, corporate bonds, auto loans — so when the 30-year Treasury moves, it moves the cost of capital for the whole economy. This week we saw that transmission directly: the same yield pressure that hit Treasuries pushed the 30-year mortgage rate above 7% for the first time since January 2025.

**Q: Stocks were up this week while bonds sold off hard. Isn't that unusual?**
A: It's unusual for it to be this pronounced, and it's worth noting the rally was narrow — the Nasdaq was up over 2% while the small-cap Russell 2000 actually fell. Large, cash-rich companies with strong earnings (like Meta, on its AI agent launch) can shrug off higher borrowing costs in a way that smaller, more debt-dependent companies can't, which is exactly the divergence we saw this week.

**Q: How should a market that trades on U.S. data every day think about a local story like Butte's data-center ballot initiative?**
A: It's a useful reminder that AI capital expenditure — one of the biggest stories in markets right now — ultimately has to land somewhere physically, and that "somewhere" increasingly has its own politics around power costs and local control. Butte's fight this week is a small-scale version of a debate that's happening in data-center hubs across the country.

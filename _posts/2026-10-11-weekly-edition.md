Stocks notched a fresh record mid-week, yields drifted down off two-decade highs, and the mortgage rate kept climbing anyway. Bank earnings season opens Tuesday, September CPI lands Wednesday, and the Fed meets in less than three weeks.

## The week in 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,811.54, up 1.15% on the week and a fresh record Tuesday before pulling back Wednesday and Thursday.
- The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.22%, down 2 bp on the week but still up 42 bp on the month.
- The 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 7.40%, up 12 bp on the week, its highest in about four years.
- Innodata fell 11.46% on the week as investors reassessed its AI-demand story; BlackBerry gave back 4.19% after its own run.
- No government shutdown this month: a continuing resolution signed September 2 funds Washington through December 11.

### Bonds

**Yields eased off two-decade highs, but just barely.** The 10-year fell 2 bp to 5.22%; the 2-year dropped 3 bp to 4.75%. Both are still up 35-42 bp on the month after the Fed's September hike to 3.75%-4.00%, its first increase since 2023, approved 12-0 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/interest-rate/news/590264)). Minutes released October 7 showed officials still see another 2026 hike as likely appropriate, timing undecided ([ROIC.ai](https://www.roic.ai/news/fed-minutes-signal-another-2026-hike-likely-appropriate-but-timing-remains-uncertain-10-07-2026)). *Markets: a Fed that's still hiking, not cutting, is why mortgage rates haven't followed the 10-year's small pullback.*

### Stocks

**The S&P set a record Tuesday, then gave half of it back.** It closed at 7,818.93 Tuesday, a third straight record, before falling Wednesday and Thursday on no single catalyst. Friday's 0.6% bounce to 7,811.54 left it up 1.15% on the week and over 13% year-to-date ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-10-09/wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-headline-busy-markets-week-sp-500)).

**Earnings season opens Tuesday.** JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo report Tuesday; Morgan Stanley and Bank of America follow Wednesday. LSEG-polled analysts expect S&P 500 Q3 earnings up over 30% year-over-year, a high bar after a blowout Q2 ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-10-09/wall-st-week-ahead-bank-earnings-cpi-headline-busy-markets-week-sp-500)).

### Housing

**Mortgage rates keep setting four-year highs.** The 30-year averaged 7.40% this week, up 12 bp, and up 69 bp over the past month, as the broader bond selloff and September's Fed hike both push borrowing costs higher ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/30-year-mortgage-rate/news/588887)).

### Tariffs & Trade

**No new escalation this week, but no relief either.** Section 232 tariffs on lumber (10%) and cabinets and furniture (25%) stay at their 2025 levels after the White House delayed a scheduled jump to 30%/50% by a full year, to January 2027, citing ongoing trade talks ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-tariff-increase-delay-furniture-kitchen-cabinets)). *Markets: it's relief for homebuilders and furniture retailers already squeezed by 7%-plus mortgages, but a one-year reprieve, not a resolution.*

### Commodities

**Oil held flat.** WTI crude is $96.24/bbl, unchanged on the week but up 3.83% on the month, near its highest since the Iran war began.

**Silver has cooled from its January record.** It traded near $61/oz this week, roughly half its late-January peak of $121/oz, after a run tied to industrial demand and safe-haven buying ([MarketScreener](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/record-start-to-2026-brings-prospect-of-5-000-gold-into-view-ce7e58dbdc8ef221)).

## Key Economies

### Japan

The Bank of Japan meets October 29-30, its first quarterly Outlook Report since raising its rate to 1.25% in September, a 31-year high, on a split 7-2 vote. The yen sold off on the move instead of strengthening; investors had priced in the hike and focused on the roughly 2.75-point rate gap still open with the Fed ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/th-boj-rate-decision-1-25-why-yen-falling)). *Markets: a weaker yen despite a hike keeps Japanese exporters competitive even as home borrowing costs rise.*

### United Kingdom

The Bank of England held its rate at 3.75% on September 17, a 6-3 vote, with the three dissenters wanting 4.00% as August inflation ran 3.1% ([data](https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/10990/)). The UK's next GDP estimate, covering Q2, lands Thursday, October 15. *Markets: a split, inflation-wary board leaves little room for cuts even as growth stays weak.*

### China

Mainland markets reopened October 8 after the week-long National Day holiday. Earlier 2026 data showed exports up roughly 27% year-over-year in June on EV, battery, and AI-hardware demand, even as shipments to the U.S. specifically fell and exports to Southeast Asia, Africa, and the EU picked up the slack ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/chinas-trade-growth-remains-robust-in-2026-driven-by-high-value-exports/)). *Markets: that shift away from the U.S. is the clearest sign tariffs are rerouting trade, not halting it.*

## Politics & Geopolitics

### Conflicts

**Gaza ceasefire passes its one-year mark under strain.** The truce Trump announced in October 2025 reached its anniversary this week. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called for a permanent ceasefire, saying the current one has "yet to deliver security, adequate humanitarian assistance or a credible path to recovery." More than 1,000 Palestinians have been killed in Gaza since the ceasefire took hold, by health ministry counts, and Israel still controls a buffer zone covering over 60% of the territory pending verified Hamas disarmament ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/un-chief-gaza-ceasefire-ahead-of-october-7-anniversary-2026)). *Markets: a fragile-but-holding ceasefire has kept oil's war premium contained near $96/bbl.*

### U.S. National Politics

**No shutdown, unlike a year ago.** The government is funded through December 11 under a continuing resolution the House passed 370-48 and Trump signed September 2, avoiding the lapse that hit in October 2025. Lawmakers still must pass full-year FY2027 appropriations, or another stopgap, before then ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_federal_government_shutdowns)). *Markets: stable funding means the Fed gets its usual jobs and inflation data heading into the October 28-29 meeting.*

### Global Affairs

**Ukraine peace talks remain stuck on Donbas.** The most recent detailed reporting on U.S.-brokered talks has Russia insisting Ukrainian forces fully withdraw from Donetsk and Luhansk, while Ukraine has proposed a demilitarized zone instead. Postwar security guarantees and ceasefire sequencing are also unresolved ([Yahoo/AP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/trump-putin-call-hosts-zelenskyy-193258824.html)). *Markets: a deal would likely ease oil's supply-risk premium; no resolution this week means it stays priced in.*

## Montana & Bozeman

### Housing

The most recent city-level data (published in April, still the latest on record) put Bozeman's median single-family home price at $715,000, below the 2023 peak of $898,000, while multifamily vacancy has eased to about 12% from 18% a year ago as new apartment supply caught up with demand. Mayor Joey Morrison said the market has reached "stability," but called it a stability that's "not a good" one given persistent unaffordability ([Montana Free Press](https://montanafreepress.org/2026/04/03/bozeman-housing-market-cools-but-a-stable-market-hasnt-meant-an-affordable-one/)).

### Business

Governor Gianforte and the Montana Chamber of Commerce co-host the Big Sky, Bright Future Summit October 14-15, the state's marquee business-outlook event, days after Glacier Bancorp's dividend raise put the Kalispell-based bank back in the news ([Montana Chamber](https://montanachamber.com/all-events/)). Montana's business-formation boom has continued through the year, with the Secretary of State's office reporting more than 21,000 new business registrations filed since January, including a record 8,000-plus launched in March alone ([Secretary of State](https://sosmt.gov/secretary-of-state-christi-jacobsen-announces-montanas-record-breaking-march-over-8000-new-businesses-launched/)).

## Connecting the dots

- The Fed hiked in September specifically because inflation was too high, yet the 30-year mortgage keeps setting four-year highs and stocks keep setting records anyway; something eventually has to reconcile tighter policy with looser financial conditions.
- No shutdown this October, versus one a year ago, means the Fed walks into its October 28-29 meeting with real jobs and inflation data for the first time in two cycles, raising the stakes on Wednesday's CPI print.
- A tariff delay on furniture and cabinets, a yen that's falling despite a BOJ hike, and China's export shift away from the U.S. are all the same story from different angles: trade policy is reshaping flows, not stopping them, and nobody involved is getting a clean win.

## Questions to think about

- If the Fed is still hiking to fight inflation, why are stocks still setting records? Is the market pricing in a soft landing, or just ignoring the risk?
- Mortgage rates have now topped 7% for most of a year. At what point does that become a structural feature of the housing market rather than a temporary squeeze?
- Gaza's ceasefire has held for a year without delivering peace. What would it take for "no war" to become "actual peace," and is that even the right bar to measure against?

## Interview angle

**"Why is the Fed hiking while other central banks cut or hold?"** The Fed raised its range to 3.75%-4.00% in September because U.S. inflation, near 3.4%-3.7%, sits well above its 2% target. The Bank of England held at 3.75% this month with inflation at 3.1% and growth weaker; the Bank of Japan just hiked to a 31-year high of 1.25% after decades near zero. Each bank is reacting to its own inflation and growth mix, not a shared script.

**"What's the signal in mortgage rates hitting four-year highs while stocks hit records?"** The economy is running on two speeds. Rate-sensitive housing is under real strain, while earnings, helped by resilient spending and AI capex, keep beating estimates. That gap can persist for a while, but it's also a classic setup for volatility if credit costs or labor data turn.

**"How do you think about tariff risk right now?"** Policy keeps shifting, this week's one-year delay on furniture and cabinet duties is a good example, so model exposure under current rates, a further delay, and a full escalation rather than betting on one outcome. China's trade data shows the broader lesson: trade is rerouting around tariffs, not waiting them out.

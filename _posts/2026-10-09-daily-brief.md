Innodata extended its slide to almost 15% on the week, the 30-year mortgage hit a fresh four-year high, and stocks pulled back for a second day from Tuesday's record.

## In 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 fell 0.47% Thursday to 7,765.36, its second straight down day after Tuesday's record close.
- Innodata dropped another 3.5% to $61.20, now down 14.82% this week as the AI-data trade keeps unwinding.
- The 30-year mortgage rate jumped 12 bp to 7.40%, its highest level in roughly four years.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-09/markets.png)

**Stocks**: The S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36 Thursday, the Nasdaq dropped 1.25% to 27,193.34, and the Dow edged up 0.10% to 51,231.64. All three are still higher on the week, led by the S&P at +1.29%.

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield is 5.28%, roughly flat on the week but up 50 bp over the past month to near two-decade highs. The 2-year sits at 4.77% (down 11 bp this week) and the 30-year at 5.67% (up 3 bp this week). September CPI lands October 14, ahead of the Fed's October 28 decision.

**Housing**: The average 30-year mortgage rate rose to 7.40% this week, up 12 bp, its highest level in roughly four years, as the bond selloff keeps pushing borrowing costs higher ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/30-year-mortgage-rate/news/588887)).

**Commodities**: WTI crude is $96.24/bbl, up 3.83% on the month.

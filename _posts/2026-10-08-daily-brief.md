Stocks eased off Tuesday's record, BlackBerry and Innodata both slid after big runs, and the next catalysts are CPI on October 14 and the Fed on October 28.

## In 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 slipped 0.22% to 7,801.77 Wednesday, pulling back from Tuesday's record close.
- BlackBerry fell 8.46% and Innodata dropped 4.26%, both giving back part of recent post-earnings and AI-demand rallies.
- The 30-year mortgage rate held at a four-year high of 7.28%; the next Fed decision is October 28, with September CPI due October 14.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-08/markets.png)

**Stocks**: The S&P 500 fell 0.22% to 7,801.77 Wednesday, the Nasdaq slipped 0.22% to 27,538.69, and the Dow dropped 0.66% to 51,179.87. The S&P and Nasdaq are still up 1-4% on the month; the Dow is down 4.18% over that span.

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield is 5.27%, down 4 bp on the day but up 49 bp over the month. The 2-year sits at 4.79% (down 10 bp this week) and the 30-year at 5.64% (up 5 bp this week). The Fed raised its target range to 3.75%-4.00% in September to fight inflation; September CPI lands October 14 (consensus 3.7% year-over-year, up from 3.4%), ahead of the Fed's October 28 decision ([nowflation](https://nowflation.com/cpi-release-dates)).

**Housing**: The average 30-year mortgage rate is 7.28% (Freddie Mac, as of October 1), up 25 bp this week and 62 bp this month, its highest level in about four years, as the bond selloff and September's rate hike keep pushing borrowing costs up ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/30-year-mortgage-rate/news/588887)).

**Commodities**: WTI crude is $96.24/bbl, up 3.83% on the month.

## Watchlist

![Watchlist]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-08/watchlist.png)

**BB -8.46%** to $8.77: BlackBerry gave back part of this week's rally. Tuesday's fiscal Q2 beat (revenue $163.3 million, up 26%; adjusted EPS $0.07 versus $0.04 expected) had pushed shares to $9.58, but Canaccord trimmed its price target to $9.50 from $10.30 on valuation, and the stock pulled back Wednesday ([StocksToTrade](https://stockstotrade.com/news/blackberry-limited-bb-news-2026_10_06/)).

**INOD -4.26%** to $63.42: Innodata slid as investors refocused on customer concentration — two clients still account for roughly 70% of revenue — after a rally that left the stock up nearly 14% on the month ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4937245-innodata-stock-priced-for-perfection-customer-concentration-raises-the-risk)).

Quiet: JPM, GBCI.

## World

**Japan**: The Bank of Japan holds its next policy meeting October 29-30, its first full quarterly Outlook Report since raising rates to 1.25% in September, a 31-year high. The board was split 7-2 on that move, and economists are divided on whether another hike follows ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/world/40071184)).

## Politics & Geopolitics

**No shutdown**: The federal government is funded through December 11 under a continuing resolution signed September 2; lawmakers still need to pass the twelve annual appropriations bills, or another stopgap, by that deadline. *Markets: stable funding removes a source of uncertainty for Treasury auctions and economic-data releases heading into the Fed's October 28 meeting.* ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/house-passes-funding-stopgap-averting-government-shutdown-in-october/))

## One question

The Fed hiked in September to fight inflation, mortgages sit at a four-year high, and stocks keep setting records anyway. What breaks that pattern first: inflation data, or earnings?

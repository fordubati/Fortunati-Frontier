The Fed held rates in a rare 10-2 split, with Chair Kevin Warsh refusing to commit to December. Israel's election left Netanyahu and challenger Gadi Eisenkot both short of a majority, with Likud alleging the vote was "stolen." Innodata crashed 25% on layoff news and never recovered. The Bank of Japan held but turned more hawkish. And in two days, Montana's Western District race and the full U.S. midterms decide who runs Washington.

## The week in 60 seconds

- The Fed held its rate at 3.75%-4.00% Wednesday in a 10-2 vote; Governors Cook and Hammack dissented, wanting a hike, and Chair Warsh offered "no firm conclusions" on December.
- Israel's election ended deadlocked: Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats, Eisenkot's 48, and Arab-led parties a record 19, with no bloc at the 61 needed; Likud claims, without evidence, that the vote was "stolen."
- Innodata (INOD) crashed 25% Tuesday on reports of roughly 550 India layoffs and more cuts in the Philippines, ending the week down 26% despite a brief Wednesday bounce.
- The Bank of Japan held its rate at 1.25% in a hawkish 8-1 vote, with the statement pledging to "continue to raise" rates; dissenter Takata Hajime wanted 1.5% now.
- Hormuz tanker attacks hit a record 28 in October, the worst month since the conflict began, as Montana's toss-up Western District race and the full midterms arrive Tuesday.

### Bonds

**The Fed held its target rate at 3.75%-4.00% Wednesday, a 10-2 vote.** Governors Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissented, wanting a quarter-point hike instead; the statement called activity "strong" with inflation "elevated." [Read more →](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a1.htm)

**Chair Kevin Warsh won't commit to a December move.** He said September's hike "is starting to work some" but called inflation too high, adding there are "no firm conclusions now about monetary policy." The 10-year yield ended at 4.98%, up 2 bp on the week even as the 2-year fell 4 bp, a mild steepening. [Read more →](https://www.stonex.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/fomc-recap-fed-holds-but-warsh-falls-short-of-a-full-hawkish-hold/)

### Stocks

Innodata's 26% plunge was the week's biggest single-stock mover. [Read more →](https://finance.biggo.com/news/8ab817e1-258c-47c0-8bad-6f17b6bfe3df)

### Housing

**Builder confidence fell to its lowest since June 2012.** The NAHB/Wells Fargo index dropped for a second straight month to 29 in October, from 32 in September; 43% of builders cut prices, the highest share since 2020. [Read more →](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/nahb-housing-market-index/news/593166)

## Politics & Geopolitics

### Conflicts

**Hormuz tanker attacks hit a record 28 in October, the worst month since the war began.** The Washington Institute's Noam Raydan said Iran is intensifying strikes to slow shipping and pressure energy markets ahead of Tuesday's U.S. midterms; a dozen ships were hit in one week alone, the highest weekly total of the conflict. [Read more →](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/10/ship-attacks-strait-hormuz-hit-record-high-october)

**Israel's deadlocked election leaves the Iran-strike question unresolved**, since any renewed Israeli move on Iran stays on hold until a government forms. *Markets: a drawn-out coalition fight keeps the Hormuz risk premium in place.* [Read more →](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/israel-election-netanyahu-eisenkot-coalition-talks-.html)

### U.S. National Politics

**The midterms are two days out.** All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats, and 36 governorships are on the ballot Tuesday. Nonpartisan forecasters still favor a Democratic House, while the Senate remains a closer call: Cook Political Report and Sabato's Crystal Ball both lean Democratic in the House, while Senate models range from a 74% Democratic edge to a near-coin-flip. [Read more →](https://www.news9.com/national-politics-oklahoma/one-week-away-the-stakes-if-the-midterm-elections-loom-large)

**Montana's Western District House race remains a true toss-up heading into Election Day.** Democrat Sam Forstag and Republican Aaron Flint have essentially tied in every poll since June, and the seat, vacated by Ryan Zinke, has drawn a parade of national surrogates for Flint and heavy outside spending for both sides. [Read more →](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/10/17/analysts-shift-western-montana-congressional-district-to-toss-up-status/)

### Global Affairs

**Likud alleges the election was "stolen," without evidence, as the shekel and Israeli stocks take a hit.** The shekel had its worst intraday drop since 2023 before recovering, and Israeli stocks fell 4.1% on the week. Results go to President Herzog next Wednesday, with the new Knesset sworn in November 10. *Markets: prolonged coalition talks mean weeks more uncertainty for Israeli assets.* [Read more →](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/israel-election-netanyahu-eisenkot-coalition-talks-.html)

## Connecting the dots

- A hawkish BOJ and a Fed that won't commit to December point the same direction: central banks are more worried about sticky inflation than slowing growth, even as Treasury yields barely moved.
- Oil fell even as Hormuz attacks hit a record, a sign markets may be pricing in the risk already, or betting a drawn-out Israeli coalition fight delays any escalation.
- Builder confidence at a 14-year low and pending sales at a record low both reflect a market locked up by mortgage rates above 7%, even as those rates have eased two weeks running.

## Questions to think about

- If Israel's coalition talks drag on for weeks, as past ones have taken 20 to 100 days, does that delay the risk markets have been pricing into oil, or just relocate it?
- The Fed held but two governors dissented toward a hike, and the BOJ held but turned more hawkish. Are central banks converging on the same inflation worry, or reacting to different pressures that happen to look similar?
- How long a gap can open between the price of borrowing and actual housing activity before one side gives?

## Interview angle

**"Why didn't the Fed's hold calm markets this week?"** Two governors dissented toward a hike, and Chair Warsh said there are "no firm conclusions" on December, leaving markets with the same uncertainty they had going in.

**"What does Israel's deadlocked election mean for markets?"** With neither bloc at the 61-seat majority, coalition talks could take weeks, keeping Israeli stocks and the shekel under pressure and leaving any Israeli move on Iran unresolved.

**"Why is Innodata down 26% even after bouncing mid-week?"** Reports of roughly 550 layoffs in India, with more cuts in the Philippines, raised questions about the AI-data company's growth story; the bounce didn't hold once the market digested the news, with Q3 earnings due around November 5.

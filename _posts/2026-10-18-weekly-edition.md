A Friday selloff in chip stocks erased the week's gains, bank earnings split for a third straight week, and Fed Chair Warsh declined to tip his hand on October at an IMF meeting in Bangkok. Oil held near $102, China's exports kept growing despite tariffs, and the midterms are two and a half weeks out.

## The week in 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 fell 1.95% on the week to 7,659.44, its worst week since June, as a Friday chip-stock selloff (Micron -5.6%, Intel -5.8%) wiped out an early-week bounce.
- Bank earnings split again: JPMorgan and Wells Fargo missed on revenue; Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Morgan Stanley, and Truist beat; Bank of America's profit fell 17% on higher costs.
- The 10-year Treasury yield fell 12 bp to 5.10% even as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh declined to signal an October hike at a Bangkok fireside chat with the IMF's Kristalina Georgieva.
- WTI crude held near $101.91/bbl, up 7.25% on the week, as the Strait of Hormuz standoff drags on; China's rare-earth truce expires in three weeks, on November 10.
- INOD and BB both resumed last month's slide, down 14.86% and 9.54% with no fresh company news behind either move, while JPM and GBCI quietly outperformed.

### Bonds

**The Fed's Beige Book flagged prices, not growth, as the worry.** Released Wednesday, nine of 12 districts reported some growth, while most called tariff, labor, and energy price pressure "moderate" or stronger, through October 5 ([Minneapolis Fed](https://minneapolisfed.org/beige-book-reports/2026/2026-10-su)). Fed Chair Warsh stayed quiet at an IMF fireside chat in Bangkok Thursday, declining to say whether the Fed hikes October 27-28 while repeating that inflation is still too high ([CoinGape](https://coingape.com/key-takeaways-from-fed-chair-kevin-warshs-imf-fireside-chat-as-bitcoin-xrp-hold-gains/)).

### Stocks

**Bank earnings split for a third straight week.** JPMorgan and Wells Fargo missed on revenue despite beating on EPS; Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Truist beat on both lines, though Citigroup's stock fell despite a 40% profit jump on slower trading revenue ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000072971/000007297126000318/wfc3qer10-13x26ex991xrelea.htm)). A chip-stock selloff gave the week its direction: Micron fell 5.6% and Intel 5.8% Friday on AI-buildout pace doubts, dragging the S&P down 1.1% that day ([AP via La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-10162026-nid16102026/)).

### Housing

**The mortgage rate held at a four-year high.** The 30-year averaged 7.40%, unchanged on the week. August building permits fell 11.9% from July to a 1,394,000 annual rate, 27.1% below a year ago; the September report is due October 20 ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/pdf/newresconst_202608.pdf)).

### Tariffs & Trade

**China's rare-earth truce has company.** Beijing's one-year pause on expanded export controls for five rare-earth elements expires November 10. A separate suspension on tighter US-bound licensing for gallium, germanium, antimony, and graphite runs to November 27 ([China Briefing](https://china-briefing.com/news/chinas-rare-earth-export-controls)). *Markets: two deadlines now sit on the calendar for anyone sourcing Chinese inputs.*

### Commodities

**Oil stayed near its highest levels of the year.** WTI crude is $101.91/bbl, up 7.25% on the week, as fighting around the Strait of Hormuz keeps shipping risk elevated. Gold cooled by contrast: spot traded near $4,159/oz October 5, below January's record, as higher yields dulled its appeal ([Arab Finance](https://www.arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/gold-prices-hold-steady-on-october-5)).

## Key Economies

### Japan

The Bank of Japan meets October 29-30, its next Outlook Report since September's 1.25% rate hike, a 31-year high passed 7-2. Markets are watching for any signal on a second 2026 hike. *Markets: a hawkish BOJ narrows the gap with the Fed, the main force behind the yen's weakness this year.*

### United Kingdom

The UK's most recent GDP read, revised September 30, showed the economy grew 0.5% quarter-on-quarter in Q2, led by exports. The Bank of England holds at 3.75% with its next decision November 5 ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/gdp-growth/news/588004)). *Markets: steady growth and above-target inflation both argue against a near-term BOE cut.*

### China

September trade data, released Wednesday, showed exports up 36.3% year-over-year to $447.18 billion and imports up 31%, both well above forecasts; the trade surplus hit $133.53 billion ([SCMP](https://scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3370799/chinas-trade-momentum-quickens-september-amid-tech-demand-holiday-rush)). *Markets: tariffs are rerouting China's trade, not shrinking it, the same pattern that makes the November 10 rare-earth deadline worth watching.*

## Politics & Geopolitics

### Conflicts

**The Strait of Hormuz standoff shows no sign of resolving.** Iran's naval forces and U.S. Central Command continue to give conflicting accounts of whether the strait is open, with the dispute running for months and keeping a war premium in oil prices. *Markets: that unresolved risk is the main reason WTI holds near $102 instead of retreating with other commodities.*

### U.S. National Politics

**The midterms are two and a half weeks away.** All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats, and 36 governorships are on the November 3 ballot. The House is split 220-213 for Republicans, and the Cook Political Report rates 17 of 66 competitive races as true toss-ups ([Yahoo/AP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/u-house-contests-decide-control-140000554.html)). *Markets: control of Congress shapes the FY2027 budget fight pending before the December 11 funding deadline.*

### Global Affairs

**Trilateral Ukraine talks remain more planned than scheduled.** Kyiv and the Kremlin both floated October as a window for US-brokered three-way talks after visits from envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, but no session was confirmed this week, and Kyiv's own officials remain skeptical a deal is close ([Global Banking and Finance](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/kyiv-preparing-talks-resume-october-senior-ukrainian/)). *Markets: no breakthrough means oil's geopolitical risk premium stays priced in alongside Hormuz.*

## Connecting the dots

- Banks keep splitting the same way: fee-heavy units (Citi, Goldman, Morgan Stanley) are carrying the quarter while lending-heavy balance sheets (JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Bank of America) miss on revenue or get squeezed by costs.
- A chip-stock selloff, not Fed guidance or last week's CPI print, set this week's direction, evidence that AI-capex doubts can now move markets faster than monetary-policy signals.
- China's exports grew 36% in September despite Washington's tariffs, the same "reroute, don't retreat" pattern as last month, while Beijing's biggest leverage point, the rare-earth truce, still lapses in three weeks.

## Questions to think about

- If one day of chip-selloff news can erase a week's gains, how much of the market's 2026 run is really priced on a handful of semiconductor forecasts?
- Banks keep reporting the same split: strong fees, weaker lending economics. At what point does that become the new normal instead of a quarter-to-quarter story?
- If the rare-earth truce lapses November 10 without a deal, who absorbs the compliance cost first, manufacturers or the consumers buying what they make?

## Interview angle

**"Why did stocks fall when most big banks beat earnings?"** The market had priced in bank earnings already; Friday's chip-stock selloff on AI-capex doubts set the week's direction instead.

**"What's the read on the Fed after this week?"** Warsh avoided tipping his hand on an October hike in Bangkok, and the Beige Book showed moderating growth alongside still-elevated prices, a mixed picture ahead of October 27-28.

**"How should investors think about China's trade data given tariffs?"** September exports rose 36% even with US tariffs in place, showing exporters are rerouting volume, not losing it. The bigger swing factor is whether the rare-earth truce gets extended before lapsing November 10.

Democrats swept Congress Tuesday, flipping seven Senate seats and Montana's Western District for their widest House majority since 1993. The relief didn't last: a Thursday rout in AI and chip stocks sent the Nasdaq into correction, its worst day in 19 months. Innodata beat big on earnings that same night but fell anyway, the Bank of England raised rates for the first time in three years, and two Senate races still aren't called.

## The week in 60 seconds

- Democrats swept control of Congress Tuesday, flipping seven Senate seats for their widest House majority since 1993; Montana's Western District flipped too, with Democrat Sam Forstag beating Republican Aaron Flint 132,003 to 122,694.
- A week later, two Senate races remain uncalled: Alaska, where Mary Peltola and Dan Sullivan are split by roughly 600 votes and the Justice Department has sued to block certification if Peltola wins; and Mississippi, where Democrat Scott Colom leads Cindy Hyde-Smith by a few dozen votes out of nearly a million cast.
- The Nasdaq fell into correction Thursday, down 5.5% in its worst session in 19 months as AI and chip stocks led a rout, then clawed back 1.09% Friday to 24,579.42; it still finished the week down 3.37%.
- The Bank of England raised its key rate to 4.00% Wednesday, an 8-1 vote and its first hike in three years, even as the Fed stays undecided on December.
- Innodata beat big on Q3 earnings Thursday night (revenue up 53% to $95.6 million, raised guidance) but closed the week down 15.5% as the broader AI selloff swallowed the news.

## Connecting the dots

- A hiking Bank of England and a still-undecided Fed are reading similar inflation data differently: the BoE moved first, while the Fed, facing a cooler U.S. labor market, is still weighing a December hike or hold.
- Thursday's AI-stock rout hit Tokyo nearly as hard as New York, a sign this is a global repricing of AI-capex risk, not a U.S.-only story, even as the same flight to safety pulled down oil and yields together.
- Two uncalled Senate races and a few dozen votes in Mississippi show how close an otherwise lopsided "blue tsunami" election actually was in the states that mattered most.

## Questions to think about

- If the AI-stock selloff is a healthy repricing rather than a bubble bursting, what would show up in December's jobs and inflation data to tell the difference?
- Does a Democratic Congress actually change the pace of the AI buildout, or does Washington mostly react to spending decisions tech companies already made?
- With the BoE hiking and the Fed still undecided, is the U.S. unusually behind on inflation, or unusually exposed to a slowing labor market that other economies don't share?

## Interview angle

**"Why did the Nasdaq fall into correction the same week Democrats won Congress?"** The two aren't directly connected: Thursday's rout was led by chip and AI stocks as investors demanded proof the AI buildout pays off, a worry that had been building for weeks; the election added a secondary worry about data-center regulation under a Democratic House.

**"How can a race be called nationally while two Senate seats stay uncalled a week later?"** The AP calls races once the math is certain; Alaska needs a ranked-choice runoff since no candidate cleared 50%, and Mississippi's margin is inside the range a recount or legal challenge could still flip.

**"Why did Innodata's stock fall after a blowout quarter?"** Earnings landed Thursday night, hours after the market had already sold off every AI-adjacent stock that day; the beat wasn't repriced by Friday's close, so the stock finished the week down 15.5% despite 53% revenue growth and raised guidance.

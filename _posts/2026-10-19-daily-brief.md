Chip stocks set Friday's tone after a Financial Times report showed OpenAI's revenue running well below expectations. The S&P fell for a second straight week, and the watchlist's INOD and BB extended their slide. Cook Political Report moved 11 House races toward Democrats.

## In 60 seconds

- The S&P 500 fell 1.09% Friday to 7,659.44, closing the week down 1.95% on an OpenAI-driven chip-stock selloff.
- INOD dropped 6.63% to $52.82 and BB fell 7.36% to $8.06, both extending recent slides with no fresh company news behind the moves.
- Cook Political Report shifted 11 House races toward Democrats Friday, three from solid to likely Republican.

## Markets

![Markets, last 3 months]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/charts/2026-10-19/markets.png)

**Stocks**: Friday's selloff traced back to a Financial Times report, out October 13-15, that OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate was about $50 billion as of September 30, far below the $68-70 billion the market expected. Micron and Nvidia led chip stocks lower; 20 major AI and semiconductor names lost over $1 trillion combined on the week. The S&P fell 1.09% to 7,659.44, down 1.95% on the week; the Dow, less exposed to chips, dipped 0.34% to 52,002.41 but still finished the week up 0.67%; the Nasdaq closed at 26,713.03, down 1.77% on the week. [Read more →](https://cryptobriefing.com/micron-nvidia-pressure-ai-spending-fears/)

**Bonds**: The 10-year Treasury yield is 5.10%, down 9 bp on the day and 12 bp on the week. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's refusal last week to preview the October 27-28 meeting ("I don't prejudge meetings") has traders scaling back bets on a hike this month. [Read more →](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/interest-rate/news/592678)

**Housing**: The 30-year mortgage rate held at 7.40% for an eighth straight week, the longest stretch without a decline, as it sits at its highest level since November 2023. September housing starts are due Tuesday. [Read more →](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/30-year-mortgage-rate/news/588887)

## Politics & Geopolitics

**Cook Political Report shifted 11 House races toward Democrats Friday.** Three moved from solid Republican to likely Republican and five from likely Republican to lean Republican; the ratings firm had already called the House for Democrats in late September. Senate races stayed close: Georgia's Jon Ossoff leads Mike Collins by nearly 9 points in the RealClearPolitics average, while Iowa and Texas remain tight.
